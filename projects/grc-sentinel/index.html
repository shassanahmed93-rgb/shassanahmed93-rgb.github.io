GRC APPLICATION / CASE STUDY

GRC Sentinel

A risk score matters when someone can see what supports it, who owns the next action, and when to review the decision. I built GRC Sentinel to connect those steps in one workflow.

Private project. The source repository is private. The authenticated demo is a local test environment. This page is a public project walkthrough.

What I built

Risk and treatment

Score inherent and residual risk, compare them on 5×5 heat maps, and flag risks above the organization’s appetite. Track treatment actions, owners, and deadlines.

Evidence integrity

Attach evidence to controls, risks, and audit workpapers. Preserve file versions, verify integrity with SHA-256, and track review dates and expiry.

Audit and remediation

Follow audit workpapers, evidence requests, and findings through review. Record risk acceptance with an approver’s rationale and an expiry date.

Access and demo isolation

Use role-based access and organization selection in the authenticated workflow. The local demo uses its own SQLite database and evidence storage.

How the workflow fits together

Follow the record from the original risk to the final review. This example uses fictional inputs to show what someone would enter, what GRC Sentinel records, and what a reviewer can check.

1. Define the risk and the test

Record the affected cloud role, its owner, and the control that calls for least privilege. The assessment checks whether the role grants more access than the job requires.

Output: a risk record with an inherent score of 20/25 and a linked control to review.

2. Collect and verify evidence

Attach the current IAM policy and a dated access review. GRC Sentinel records the evidence version, review date, and SHA-256 digest so a reviewer can verify file integrity and see which version informed the assessment.

Output: traceable evidence linked to the control and workpaper.

3. Record the gap and assign treatment

If the policy includes unnecessary privileges, document the finding in the workpaper. Assign an owner and deadline to narrow the permissions, then retain the updated policy as a new evidence version.

Output: a finding and a treatment action with an owner, due date, and status.

4. Retest and record the decision

Compare the updated policy against the same control and reassess the remaining exposure. In this example, the residual score drops to 10/25. Compare it with the organization’s risk appetite. If acceptance is appropriate, record the approver’s rationale and an expiry date.

Output: a residual score, review decision, and a history the next reviewer can follow.
